# Learner fit: shape the class around how this person learns

The same content lands or dies depending on format. Before writing lessons, build a short learner profile from evidence, then let it set length, cadence, tone and what each lesson ends in.

## Where to look (read-only; stop when new sources stop changing the picture)
- The user's instructions to Claude: `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`, the project's `CLAUDE.md`, `~/.claude/rules/`, `~/.claude/output-styles/`. These often state communication needs directly (for example "no walls of text", "responses are read aloud").
- Saved memories: `~/.claude/projects/*/memory/` (feedback and user files are their own corrections and preferences).
- Any profile file in the project (`*profile*.md`, `about-me.md`, `README` of a personal repo).
- A knowledge-base or meeting-notes MCP if one is connected, searched for how they say they learn, what bored them, what stuck.
- The conversation itself: what they asked for, what they pushed back on.

## What to capture (each item with its source, labelled EVIDENCE or INFERENCE)
1. Input channel that works: listening, reading, watching, talking it through, building.
2. Length that holds attention, if they ever said so.
3. What shuts them down: long reading, homework, demand language, guilt, streaks, being graded.
4. What makes things stick: a built artifact, a rule written down, a story with a person in it, a number.
5. Tools they already live in (chat, Slack, email, notes app) and ones they reject.
6. Contradictions between sources. Report them; don't smooth them over.

Never diagnose and never lean on stereotypes about a label. If the user says they have ADHD, that's context, not a recipe; their own words about what works outrank any general rule.

Write the result to `<workspace>/research/learner-profile.md`, under 120 lines, a 5-line summary at the top.

## When the evidence is thin: the default design
These defaults come from a real run where the learner confirmed them, and they're kind to most busy adults:
- **About 2 minutes of audio per lesson**, two warm voices talking to each other, plus a one-screen card. Reading stays optional.
- **One idea per lesson**, with a 2 to 3 word name.
- **Every lesson ends in one real action** in the learner's actual work, drafted before they open it, doable in 2 to 15 minutes, tagged with energy (Quick Win, Deep Focus, People, Admin) and time. Practice that ships beats exercises.
- **A rule to keep** per lesson, collected in one playbook file. That file is how the learning outlives the course.
- **Skips cost nothing.** No streaks, no catch-up list, no "welcome back."
- **Progress counts effort:** lessons heard, actions shipped, rules kept. Never outcomes the learner doesn't control.
- **Arcs of about 4 lessons**, each with its own finish, so the course is many small wins rather than one long climb.
